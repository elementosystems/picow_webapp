# PERSISTENCE

A movement built on the conviction that motion observed long enough becomes
form. That a gesture, when held by the medium that received it, accrues
into geometry. Persistence is what happens when a system refuses to
forget — when each present is shaded by every recent past, and the result
is neither a snapshot nor a film, but a third thing: a standing image
made of motion.

Visually, the philosophy lives in afterglow. A single luminous trace
drawn not where it was, but where it is now plus everything still fading.
The line is always a curve, never a corner; the curve is always
continuous, never broken. Light accumulates in regions of long dwell and
attenuates in regions of haste. Where the work pauses, it brightens;
where it moves swiftly, it ghosts. This is the central craft of the
movement: the patient calibration of decay — a thing too brief is
forgotten, a thing too persistent ossifies, and the meditative window
between the two is found only by the hand of someone who has spent years
listening to the way an image breathes.

Color is restrained to the temperature of laboratory phosphor — luminous
mint at the peak of expression, cooling through teal toward the deepest
indigo at rest. There is no white. There is no red. The palette is a
single lineage from glow to dark, calibrated to a hairsbreadth, the
product of countless evenings spent matching gradients against a tinted
viewing hood. Each band of decay placed with the certainty of a master
colorist who knows the eye more intimately than any instrument.

Typography is whispered, never spoken. Small monospaced numerals at the
rim of the field. Reference marks like surveyor's stakes. A wordmark set
in a face built for the bezels of working instruments — a single phrase,
never explanation, never paragraph. Letters integrated as architecture,
positioned with the patience of a typesetter who has set ten thousand
specimens and waits for the one that sings.

Composition is concentric and rotational. Forms emerge from harmonic
ratios and dissolve back into them. Two oscillations in tension produce
a closed figure; a slight drift in either, and the figure breathes. The
viewer cannot quite tell whether the work is static or alive — both, by
design. This is the result of meticulous tuning: every parameter
adjusted by a thousandth, then again, then again, until the figure
shimmers without insisting on attention.

The work must read as the labor of a hand that has drawn the line ten
thousand times and listens for the one moment when the line answers
back. Not the aesthetic of generation, but the aesthetic of attention —
the work of someone at the absolute peak of their craft, refining a
single gesture until the gesture becomes a geometry, and the geometry
becomes a quiet truth.

---

## Manifestation rules

- **One trace.** Never two. Multiplicity dilutes.
- **Decay is the medium.** What fades carries more weight than what is
  drawn. Tune the fade by the thousandth.
- **No white. No red.** Only the lineage from phosphor peak to indigo
  rest.
- **Type is a label, not a voice.** If the work needs words to explain
  itself, the work has not yet been finished.
- **The viewer is a participant.** Their stillness extends the line;
  their motion bends it. The piece breathes with their breath.
- **The image must be a found thing, not an invented one.** The harmonic
  ratios that close the figure already exist; the artist's role is to
  dial them in until the closure resonates, then leave the room.
